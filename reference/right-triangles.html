The relativity rule The hypotenuse never changes — it's always opposite the right angle. Opposite and adjacent swap when you switch reference angles. Always name sides after choosing your angle, never before.

One ratio → all six

Companion to Lesson 0005. A single given ratio is a blueprint: it names two sides of a right triangle, Pythagoras supplies the third, and every other function is then a read-off.

GivenNamesThird side
sin θ, csc θopposite & hypotenuseadj = √(hyp² − opp²)
cos θ, sec θadjacent & hypotenuseopp = √(hyp² − adj²)
tan θ, cot θboth legshyp = √(opp² + adj²)
Add or subtract? If the given ratio mentions the hypotenuse (sin, cos, sec, csc), you subtract under the radical. If it names two legs (tan, cot), you add. That one question settles the sign inside every Pythagoras step in this topic.

For a bare letter, take the missing side as 1: sin θ = x means opp x, hyp 1. It keeps the algebra clean and matches how answer choices are written.

Standard compositions

Each is one triangle in disguise. cos(sin⁻¹ x) reads as "the cosine of the angle whose sine is x" — draw that angle and read off the cosine. These forms appear verbatim as answer choices, so recognising them is faster than deriving them, but derive them when in doubt.

ExpressionEqualsExpressionEquals
cos(sin⁻¹ x)√(1 − x²)sin(cos⁻¹ x)√(1 − x²)
tan(sin⁻¹ x)x/√(1 − x²)tan(cos⁻¹ x)√(1 − x²)/x
sin(tan⁻¹ x)x/√(1 + x²)cos(tan⁻¹ x)1/√(1 + x²)
The minus does not always carry cos(sin⁻¹(−3/5)) = +4/5. Arcsine of a negative lands in QIV, where cosine is positive. Resolve the inner angle's quadrant first, then take the sign from there — never propagate the minus by reflex.

Applied angle vocabulary

Angle of elevationmeasured up from the horizontal to the line of sight
Angle of depressionmeasured down from the horizontal to the line of sight

Both are measured from the horizontal, never the vertical. In a two-observer figure, the angle of depression from the top equals the angle of elevation from the bottom — alternate interior angles across parallel horizontals.

Setup checklist

  1. Draw it; mark the right angle (word problems hide it — "vertical," "level ground," "plumb").
  2. Label the givens; circle the target.
  3. Name sides relative to your chosen angle.
  4. Pick the ratio mentioning both known and wanted.
  5. Solve, then sanity-check.
