Counting Solutions on an Interval

cos x = 1/2 has two solutions on [0, 2π). cos 2x = 1/2 has four. Two worked examples, then thirty-four drills.

You solved equations like these in lesson 0001, always with a plain θ inside. The moment the inside becomes 2x or 3x, one cycle's worth of solving loses you half the roots. The fix is a substitution:

  1. Name the inside: u = 2x — or 3x, or 2x − π/3, whatever sits there.
  2. Stretch the window: if x runs over [0, 2π) then u = 2x runs over [0, 4π) — two circles. This is the step people skip.
  3. Solve in u, circle by circle: find the solutions in the first circle, then add 2π for each further circle.
  4. Map back: undo the whole inside — x = (u + φ)/B.

Which means you can answer "how many solutions?" without solving anything. On [0, 2π), with B a whole number:
